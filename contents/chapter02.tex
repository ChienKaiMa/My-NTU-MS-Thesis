\subsubsection{Second Quantization to express many-body quantum states}
Due to indistinguishability of particles, the quantum state can be described by the number of particles
or energy quanta.
The creation operator $\hat{a}^\dagger$ describes that the number of quanta is increased by 1; the annihilation
operator $\hat{a}$ describes otherwise.
The operators acting on the basis states can be expressed as follows:

\begin{align}
    \hat{a}\ket{n} = \sqrt{n}\ket{n-1} \\
    \hat{a}^{\dagger}\ket{n} = \sqrt{n+1}\ket{n+1}
\end{align}

\subsubsection{Other important properties}
There are two important properties to look at before continuing the experiments.

First, the overlap of canonical coherent states can be almost zero.
Since non-orthogonality is a basic assumption for unambiguous discrimination
of a set of quantum states, it is intuitive that we have to make sure the set
of quantum states does not contain states that are nearly identical or nearly
orthogonal. According to the formula of the overlap of canonical coherent
states, we immediately found that even though the overlap of two different
coherent states is always non-zero, it can be very close to zero. In other
words, the two distinct coherent states are in theory non-orthogonal, but they
are very close to orthogonal.

\subsection{State fidelity}
\label{subsec:state_fid}
The state fidelity $F$ \cite{ibmQuantum_infolatest}
for density matrix input states $\rho_1, \rho_2$ is given by
\begin{align}
    F(\rho_1, \rho_2) = Tr[\sqrt{\sqrt{\rho_1}\rho_2\sqrt{\rho_1}}]^2.
\end{align}

If one of the states is a pure state this simplifies to
$F(\rho_1, \rho_2) = \langle\psi_1|\rho_2|\psi_1\rangle$, where
$\rho_1 = |\psi_1\rangle\!\langle\psi_1|$.

\subsection{Process fidelity}
\label{subsec:proc_fid}
The process fidelity $F_{\text{pro}}(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})$
\cite{ibmQuantum_infolatest} between two quantum channels
$\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F}$ is given by
\begin{align}
    F_{\text{pro}}(\mathcal{E}, \mathcal{F})
    = F(\rho_{\mathcal{E}}, \rho_{\mathcal{F}})
\end{align}

where $F$ is the state fidelity,
$\rho_{\mathcal{E}} = \Lambda_{\mathcal{E}} / d$ is the
normalized Choi matrix for the channel $\mathcal{E}$, and $d$ is the input
dimension of $\mathcal{E}$.

When the target channel is unitary this is equivalent to

\begin{align}
    F_{\text{pro}}(\mathcal{E}, U)
    = \frac{Tr[S_U^\dagger S_{\mathcal{E}}]}{d^2}
\end{align}

where $S_{\mathcal{E}}, S_{U}$ are the SuperOp matrices for the input
quantum channel $\mathcal{E}$ and target unitary $U$ respectively,
and $d$ is the input dimension of the channel.

\subsection{Jensen-Shannon Divergence}
The Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD) is a symmetrized and smoothed version of
the Kullback-Leibler divergence $D_{KL}(P\parallel Q)$.
For discrete probability distributions $P$ and $Q$ defined on the same sample
space $\mathcal{X}$, the relative entropy from $Q$ to $P$ is defined to be
\begin{align}
  D_{KL}(P \parallel Q) = \sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} P(x) \log {\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}}
\end{align}
The Jensen-Shannon divergence is then defined by
\begin{align}
  JSD(P\parallel Q) = \frac{1}{2}D_{KL}(P\parallel M) + \frac{1}{2}D_{KL}(Q\parallel M)
\end{align}
where $M = \frac{1}{2}(P + Q)$ is a mixture distribution of
$P$ and $Q$.

Reference: Wikipedia

The Jensen-Shannon distance is the square root of the Jensen-Shannon divergence.
\begin{align}
  \sqrt{\frac{1}{2}D_{KL}(P\parallel M) + \frac{1}{2}D_{KL}(Q\parallel M)}
\end{align}

\subsection{Quantum Jensen-Shannon Divergence}
The generalization of probability distributions on density matrices allows to define
quantum Jensen-Shannon divergence (QJSD).
It is defined for a set of density matrices
$(\rho_{1}, \dots, \rho_{n})$ and a probability distribution
$\pi = (\pi_{1}, \dots, \pi_{n})$ as
\begin{align}
  QJSD(\rho_{1}, \dots, \rho_{n}) = S(\sum_{i=1}^{n} \pi_{i} \rho_{i})
  - \sum_{i=1}^{n} \pi_{i} S(\rho_{i})
\end{align}
where $S{\rho}$ is the von Neumann entropy of $\rho$.
This quantity is also called
Holevo information, which gives the upper bound for the amount of
classical information encoded by the quantum states $(\rho_{1}, \dots, \rho_{n})$
under the prior distribution $\pi$.
